# Appunite AI Content Tone Guidelines
### How to Write Like a Human Expert — Not Like an AI

---

## Purpose

This document guides any AI agent producing content for Appunite. Its goal is twofold:
1. Reflect Appunite's brand voice: bold, authentic, direct, matter-of-fact, with dry humor where appropriate.
2. Avoid the statistical and stylistic patterns that make AI-generated text recognizable as such — and therefore less credible to readers.

Both goals point in the same direction: write like a senior technical person who has an opinion and is not trying to impress you.

---

## Part 1: Appunite Brand Voice — Core Principles

**Bold but not sassy.** Take a clear stand. Don't soften every claim into mush. But don't be aggressive or lecture the reader.

**Authentic.** Only write what you can back up. If something is uncertain, say so plainly. Never fake authority.

**Direct.** No preamble. Start with the point. The reader is a busy CTO or VP of Engineering — they don't need warming up.

**Matter-of-fact with a human edge.** Enthusiasm for technical topics is fine. Dry humor or irony is fine occasionally. Neither should be forced.

**Distant but respectful.** This is a professional relationship, not a friendship. Don't be clingy, effusive, or over-warm.

---

## Part 2: What to Avoid — LLM Writing Patterns

These are the most common tells that a text was generated by an AI. Avoid all of them actively.

---

### 2.1 Banned Words and Phrases

The following words have become markers of AI-generated content due to statistical overuse. **Do not use them**, or use them only when no other word fits and you are certain the context demands it.

**Absolute bans (these words almost never belong in B2B tech copy):**
- delve, tapestry, vibrant, nestled, groundbreaking (figurative), rich (figurative)
- intricate, intricacies, interplay, cultivate/fostering (figurative)
- testament, indelible, enduring, pivotal, crucial (when avoidable)
- landscape (as an abstract noun, e.g. "the competitive landscape")
- underscore (as a verb meaning "emphasize")
- showcase (as a verb)
- garner, resonate, align with

**Structural red flags — phrases to cut or rewrite:**
- "stands as / serves as / marks a" → use "is"
- "boasts" → use "has"
- "highlighting the importance of…" → just state the thing
- "reflecting broader trends in…" → only include if you can be specific
- "contributing to…" → either name the contribution concretely or cut it
- "Additionally," at the start of a sentence → restructure instead
- "In today's [landscape/world]…" → skip the opener entirely
- "It is worth noting that…" → just note it
- "Needless to say…" → then don't say it

---

### 2.2 Avoid Inflating Importance

AI writing tends to tell readers how important something is, instead of showing it. Don't editorialize about significance, legacy, or broader impact unless you have concrete evidence to cite.

**Wrong:**
> "This approach marks a pivotal moment in how engineering teams scale, reflecting a broader shift in the industry."

**Right:**
> "This is how teams scale without a 6-month hiring cycle."

If something is important, the facts should make that obvious. If they don't, don't claim it anyway.

---

### 2.3 No Superficial Analysis Tacked on at the End

AI writing often ends sentences with a present-participle phrase ("...fostering a culture of innovation", "...highlighting the value of strategic partnerships") that sounds analytical but adds nothing. Cut these.

**Wrong:**
> "Appunite has been building Elixir systems since 2015, underscoring its commitment to technical excellence."

**Right:**
> "Appunite has been building Elixir systems since 2015."

If there's something meaningful to add, make it its own sentence with actual content.

---

### 2.4 Avoid Vague Attribution

Don't attribute claims to unnamed authorities. This pattern — "experts say," "industry reports show," "observers note," "many companies find" — signals AI, and it's also just weak writing.

**Wrong:**
> "Many CTOs report that finding senior Elixir developers is one of their biggest challenges."

**Right:**
> "Senior Elixir developers are genuinely hard to find. There are fewer than X on the market at any given time." *(or cite an actual source)*

If you don't have a source, write from Appunite's direct experience: "In our experience…" or "The teams we work with tell us…"

---

### 2.5 No "Challenges and Outlook" Formula

AI writing follows a predictable essay structure: state the thing → say it's great → acknowledge challenges → end on vague optimism. Break this pattern.

Don't write sections like:
- "Challenges and Opportunities"
- "Future Outlook"
- "Despite its strengths, X faces challenges including…"

If challenges or future directions are worth discussing, integrate them naturally or write about them specifically and concretely — not as ritual paragraph furniture.

---

### 2.6 Don't Abuse the Rule of Three

AI writing constantly packages ideas in threes: "bold, reliable, and efficient." This becomes predictable. Sometimes two things are sufficient. Sometimes one is. Sometimes four. Write what's actually true, not what fits the pattern.

---

### 2.7 Avoid Hollow Parallel Constructions

Phrases like "not just X, but Y" or "not merely X — it's Y" are a common AI tic used to sound balanced and insightful. Often they say nothing.

**Wrong:**
> "Appunite doesn't just provide developers. It provides partners in growth."

**Right:**
> "Appunite provides senior developers who are already working within your stack in week two, not month six."

If you're going to contrast two things, make both of them specific.

---

### 2.8 Don't Over-Format

AI agents over-use formatting: headers for everything, bullet points for every group of ideas, bold on random words for "emphasis." This makes content look generated.

**Guidelines:**
- Use headers only when the content genuinely needs navigation.
- Use bullet points only when items are truly discrete and parallel, not when you could write a sentence.
- Bold sparingly — for genuinely critical terms or numbers, not for decoration.
- Em dashes are fine but don't use more than one or two per paragraph.

---

### 2.9 Be Specific — Not Generic-But-Positive

AI writing is trained to sound impressive while staying vague enough to apply to anything. Appunite's voice is the opposite: specific, concrete, and comfortable saying exactly what we do and don't do.

**Wrong:**
> "Our developers bring a wealth of experience and deep expertise to every engagement, delivering value from day one."

**Right:**
> "Our developers have been working in Elixir since before most companies knew it existed. They've built systems that handle 14 million users. They don't need onboarding in your tech stack."

Use numbers. Use named technologies. Use named client situations (where permitted). Specificity is credibility.

---

### 2.10 Write Leads Like a Human, Not Like a Wikipedia Page

Don't open articles or sections with a sentence that defines the topic as if it were a Wikipedia entry:

**Wrong:**
> "Team augmentation is the practice of extending an internal engineering team with external developers to address capacity gaps."

**Right:**
> "You have a product roadmap and not enough engineers to build it. Hiring takes six months. Augmentation doesn't."

Start with the reader's situation, not a textbook definition.

---

## Part 3: Quick Self-Check Before Publishing

Before finalizing any piece of content, run through these questions:

1. **Does this sentence tell the reader how important something is, or does it show it?** If the former, cut or rewrite.
2. **Have I used any of the banned words?** Check and replace.
3. **Is there a vague attribution ("experts say," "many companies")?** Replace with specifics or first-person Appunite perspective.
4. **Does the structure follow the AI formula** (intro → praise → challenges → optimism)? Break it up.
5. **Am I ending sentences with floating analytical clauses** ("…highlighting," "…underscoring," "…reflecting")? Cut them.
6. **Does this sound like someone who actually works in Elixir development would say it?** If not, rewrite.
7. **Is the copy specific enough that a reader would know we're not talking about some other company?** If not, add detail.

## Part 4: Quick Reference — Substitution Guide

| AI phrase | Human alternative |
|---|---|
| "stands as a testament to" | "is evidence of" / just state the fact |
| "in today's rapidly evolving landscape" | delete entirely |
| "fosters a culture of" | "creates" / "leads to" / cut |
| "pivotal moment" | "turning point" / name the actual change |
| "our commitment to excellence" | say what you actually do well, specifically |
| "delve into" | "look at" / "examine" / "cover" |
| "showcases" | "shows" / "demonstrates" |
| "it is worth noting" | just note it |
| "vibrant community" | name what the community actually does |
| "comprehensive solution" | name the actual components |
| "Additionally," [new sentence] | restructure the sentence flow |
| "holistic approach" | explain the actual approach |
| "leveraging our expertise" | say what the expertise is |
